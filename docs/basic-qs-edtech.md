Use mostly **statements** to establish authority, then attach a strong question when you want replies. For a new account with 0 followers, a pure question-led strategy can make the profile sound like it is only hosting discussion, while a statement-first approach gives people a clear point of view to react to; X-focused 2026 guidance also emphasizes hooks, perspective, and intentional conversation rather than neutral prompts alone.[^1][^2][^3]

## Best ratio

A good starting mix is about 70% statement-led posts and 30% question-led posts. That balance helps you build a recognizable lens while still creating openings for discourse, and newer-account growth advice also stresses that thoughtful replies and conversation starters matter more when paired with a distinct point of view.[^2][^4][^1]

The strongest format is usually: **statement first, question second**. That makes the post feel like leadership rather than polling, while still inviting others in.[^3][^2]

## Questions to ask

Based on your screenshot, these are strong because they are specific, non-inflammatory, and open to multiple good-faith answers:

- Should AI in schools be limited to identifying learning gaps, or should it also help shape how students are taught?[^1][^2]
- If an AI tool builds student learning profiles, what safeguards should parents and teachers expect before trusting it?[^5][^2]
- What matters more here: helping teachers save time, or making sure student data and judgment stay firmly in human hands?[^6][^1]

These work better than vague prompts like “Thoughts?” because they frame the stakes clearly. Clear framing tends to produce better replies than generic engagement bait.[^2][^3]

## Better openers

Instead of posting only a question, try formats like these:

- “AI should support teachers, not quietly redefine teaching. Where should schools draw that line?”[^3][^2]
- “A Texas district is using AI to build student learning profiles. Useful intervention tool, or the start of too much algorithmic influence in classrooms?”[^1][^2]
- “The real question is not whether AI belongs in education, but who controls its role: teachers, administrators, or vendors?”[^5][^2]

Each one opens with a viewpoint, then invites response. That is closer to thought leadership than leading with a bare question.[^2][^3]

## What to avoid

Avoid sounding like you are outsourcing the opinion to the audience on every post. Thought leadership content tends to be built through repeated core ideas, clear framing, and original interpretation, not endless open-ended asking.[^5][^2]

Also avoid yes/no questions unless the topic is genuinely binary, because they often shorten the conversation. Questions that force tradeoffs, principles, or boundaries usually create healthier discourse.[^6][^1]

## Practical rule

Use this simple rule for the first 60 days: post a claim, add context, then end with one sharp question. That gives you authority, substance, and engagement in one package.[^3][^1][^2]

A template you can reuse:
“Here’s what’s happening. Here’s why it matters. Here’s the question serious people should be asking.”

For your brand, I would lean **statement-led with a discussion tail**, not question-led from the start.
